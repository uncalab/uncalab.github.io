Research

We want to understand the mechanisms of recovery in order to develop more effective treatments tailored to each person, combining intensive language therapy, drugs and non-invasive brain stimulation.

LinesProjectsPublicationsConferencesThesesCollaborations

Research lines

Combined treatment of aphasia

Pharmacological therapy, intensive language therapy and non-invasive brain stimulation, alone or combined.

  • Pharmacological therapy
  • Intensive language therapy
  • Non-invasive stimulation (tDCS and TMS)

Verbal repetition and repetitive verbal behaviours

How we repeat, and why involuntary repetitions appear in aphasia.

  • Echolalia
  • Conduite d’approche
  • Perseveration

Accent and speech production

Accent changes after brain damage or in development, and what they reveal about speech production.

  • Foreign accent syndrome
  • Loss of regional accent
  • Accent in bilingual speakers

Cognitive deficits and neuropsychiatric symptoms in aphasia

What comes with aphasia beyond language, and its treatment.

  • Depression and apathy
  • Language anxiety
  • Attention and working memory

Language learning, linguistic expertise, imitation and brain plasticity

How we learn language and how the brain reorganises, in healthy people and in people with aphasia.

  • Word and rule learning
  • Linguistic expertise
  • Verbal imitation
  • Brain plasticity
